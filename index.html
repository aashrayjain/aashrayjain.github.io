03 / Scientific Research

Neuroimaging & Trustworthy Explainable AI (XAI).

Peer-level research addressing real-world clinical challenges in Alzheimer's Disease diagnosis via deep convolutional representations, custom loss formulations, and cross-attention fusion.

RESEARCH PAPER OCTOBER 2026 100% RECALL (MODERATE AD)

Definitive Alzheimer's Disease Stratification from Axial Brain MRI: Controlled Benchmarking, Class-Imbalance Formulations, and the ArihantQNet Hybrid Architecture

Author: Aashray Jain · M.Tech AI & ML Candidate, NIMS University Rajasthan.
Affiliation: ArihantQ — AI & Innovation Lab / NIMS Marik Institute of Computing, AI, Robotics & Cybernetics.

Abstract & Contributions: Automated classification of Alzheimer's Disease (AD) stages from structural Magnetic Resonance Imaging (sMRI) is severely impaired by severe clinical class imbalances and subtle inter-class structural boundaries. In this research:

  • Controlled Benchmarking: Evaluated 6,400 T1-weighted axial brain MRI scans partitioned into Non-Demented, Very Mild, Mild, and Moderate Demented classes using deterministic 70/15/15 stratified partitioning across ResNet50, DenseNet121, and EfficientNet-B0.
  • Loss Formulation: Formulated a Normalized Square-Root Inverse-Frequency Weighted Cross-Entropy Loss coupled with AdamW ($eta_1=0.9, eta_2=0.999$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), compressing an extreme 49.78:1 raw class skew down to 7.06:1, elevating sensitivity on the rare Moderate Demented class to 100%.
  • Novel ArihantQNet Architecture: Designed a tri-branch inductive hybrid network fusing ResNet50 (macro-contextual key), DenseNet121 (dense boundary query), and EfficientNet-B0 (channel-calibrated value) through $1 imes 1$ bottleneck projections, Quantum-Inspired Multi-Head Cross-Attention (Q-MCA), and Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) recalibration.
  • Clinical Explainability (Grad-CAM): Generated high-fidelity gradient-weighted activation maps isolating neuroanatomical hallmarks — confirming selective focal activation over bilateral hippocampal volume loss and lateral ventricular dilation.
